Why pooling fails

Fifty states do not share one emissions problem

Every state in 2023, ranked by per-capita emissions. The three at the top are not outliers in degree. They are different in kind.

Three states, three different stories

Each of the top three emitters gets its own regression on the same five predictors. The predictors that matter change completely between them.

Where these models break

Each model was refit on 1998 to 2019 alone, then asked to predict four years it had never seen. Reporting only the fit statistic would hide what happens next.

The fit statistic is not the whole story

Wyoming's model explains 98% of in-sample variance and still runs about 5 t per person high on every unseen year. A high adjusted R2 measures how well a model describes the past, not how well it forecasts.

Alaska drifts in one direction

Every held-out Alaska year is under-predicted, and the gap widens as emissions recover after 2020. A consistently signed error is structural: something outside these five predictors is doing the work.

Five predictors miss flaring

None of the five variables capture gas flared at the wellhead, which is material in the Bakken and on the North Slope. The model cannot see a source that never enters its inputs.
